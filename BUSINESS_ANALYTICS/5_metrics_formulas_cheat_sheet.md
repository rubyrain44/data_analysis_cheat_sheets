# DATA ANALYST METRICS & FORMULAS CHEAT SHEET

# 1. REVENUE & PROFIT METRICS

## Revenue

REVENUE:
Total money generated from selling products or services before deducting expenses.

Formula:

Revenue = Price × Quantity Sold

Example:
100 products × $25 = $2,500 revenue.

## Total Revenue

Total Revenue = Sum of All Sales

## Gross Profit

Gross Profit = Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

Example:
Revenue = $10,000
COGS = $4,000

Gross Profit = $6,000

## Gross Profit Margin

Gross Profit Margin = (Gross Profit / Revenue) × 100

Example:
($6,000 / $10,000) × 100 = 60%

Measures the percentage of revenue remaining after direct costs.

## Operating Profit

Operating Profit = Revenue - COGS - Operating Expenses

Measures profit generated from normal business operations before interest and taxes.

## Operating Profit Margin

Operating Profit Margin = (Operating Profit / Revenue) × 100

## Net Profit

Net Profit = Total Revenue - Total Expenses

Includes expenses such as:
- COGS.
- Operating expenses.
- Interest.
- Taxes.

## Net Profit Margin

Net Profit Margin = (Net Profit / Revenue) × 100

Measures the percentage of revenue remaining as profit after all expenses.

## Revenue Growth

Revenue Growth (%) = ((Current Revenue - Previous Revenue) / Previous Revenue) × 100

Example:
Previous Revenue = $100,000
Current Revenue = $120,000

Growth = 20%

## Percentage Change

Percentage Change = ((New Value - Old Value) / Old Value) × 100

IMPORTANT:
- Positive result = Increase.
- Negative result = Decrease.
- If the old value is zero, percentage change is undefined.

## Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

COGS = Direct Costs of Producing or Purchasing Goods Sold

Examples:
- Product materials.
- Direct production labor.
- Purchase cost of inventory.

IMPORTANT:
COGS definitions depend on the business and accounting method.

# 2. SALES & CUSTOMER METRICS

## Average Order Value (AOV)

AOV = Total Revenue / Number of Orders

Example:
Revenue = $50,000
Orders = 1,000

AOV = $50

Measures average spending per order.

## Average Selling Price (ASP)

ASP = Total Sales Revenue / Units Sold

Measures the average selling price per unit.

## Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

CAC = Total Customer Acquisition Costs / Number of New Customers Acquired

Example:
Marketing and sales costs = $10,000
New customers = 200

CAC = $50

Measures the average cost of acquiring a customer.

IMPORTANT:
Define which sales and marketing costs are included and use a consistent acquisition period.

## Customer Lifetime Value (CLV / LTV)

A measure of the estimated value a customer generates over their relationship with a business.

Simple Revenue-Based Estimate:

CLV = Average Revenue Per Customer Per Period × Average Customer Lifespan

Profit-Based Estimate:

CLV = Average Gross Profit Per Customer Per Period × Average Customer Lifespan

IMPORTANT:
More advanced CLV calculations account for retention, margins, acquisition costs and discount rates.

## Customer Retention Rate

Retention Rate (%) = ((Customers at End - New Customers Acquired) / Customers at Start) × 100

Example:
Starting Customers = 1,000
Ending Customers = 900
New Customers = 100

Retention Rate = 80%

Measures how many existing customers were retained.

## Customer Churn Rate

Churn Rate (%) = (Customers Lost During Period / Customers at Start of Period) × 100

Example:
Starting Customers = 1,000
Customers Lost = 100

Churn Rate = 10%

IMPORTANT:
Define customer churn consistently. New customers acquired during the period should not be included in the starting customer base.

## Repeat Purchase Rate

Repeat Purchase Rate (%) = (Customers With More Than One Purchase / Total Purchasing Customers) × 100

Measures the proportion of customers who purchase more than once.

## Customer Growth Rate

Customer Growth (%) = ((Ending Customers - Starting Customers) / Starting Customers) × 100

Measures the percentage change in customer count.

IMPORTANT:
Customer growth and customer acquisition are not the same. Growth also reflects customers lost.

# 3. MARKETING & CONVERSION METRICS

## Conversion Rate

Conversion Rate (%) = (Conversions / Total Visitors or Opportunities) × 100

Example:
1,000 website visitors
50 purchases

Conversion Rate = 5%

IMPORTANT:
Clearly define what counts as a conversion.

## Click-Through Rate (CTR)

CTR (%) = (Total Clicks / Total Impressions) × 100

Measures how often people click after seeing an advertisement or link.

## Cost Per Click (CPC)

CPC = Total Advertising Spend / Total Clicks

Measures average cost per click.

## Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)

CPA = Total Campaign Cost / Number of Desired Actions

Measures the average cost of generating a specified action.

IMPORTANT:
CPA and CAC can differ. CAC specifically measures acquiring customers, while CPA may measure other actions.

## Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

ROAS = Revenue Attributed to Advertising / Advertising Spend

Example:
Advertising Revenue = $20,000
Advertising Spend = $5,000

ROAS = 4

Or 4:1.

IMPORTANT:
ROAS is a revenue-to-ad-spend ratio, not profit.

## Marketing ROI

Marketing ROI (%) = ((Return Attributable to Marketing - Marketing Investment) / Marketing Investment) × 100

IMPORTANT:
Be clear about whether return means revenue, gross profit or another financial benefit.

# 4. FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE METRICS

## Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI (%) = ((Investment Return - Investment Cost) / Investment Cost) × 100

Example:
Investment = $10,000
Return = $13,000

ROI = 30%

Measures investment return relative to its cost.

## Break-Even Point

Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price Per Unit - Variable Cost Per Unit)

Example:
Fixed Costs = $10,000
Selling Price = $50
Variable Cost = $30

Break-Even Units = 500

The business must sell 500 units to cover its fixed and variable costs.

## Contribution Margin

Contribution Margin = Revenue - Variable Costs

## Contribution Margin Ratio

Contribution Margin Ratio (%) = (Contribution Margin / Revenue) × 100

Measures the percentage of revenue remaining after variable costs to cover fixed costs and profit.

# 5. IMPORTANT FINANCIAL DISTINCTIONS

REVENUE vs PROFIT:
Revenue is money generated from sales. Profit is what remains after expenses.

GROSS PROFIT vs NET PROFIT:
Gross profit subtracts COGS. Net profit accounts for all expenses.

MARGIN vs MARKUP:
Margin = Profit / Selling Price

Markup = Profit / Cost

ROI vs ROAS:
ROI evaluates investment return relative to cost. ROAS compares advertising-attributed revenue with advertising spend.

CAC vs CPA:
CAC measures customer acquisition cost. CPA can measure the cost of any defined conversion action.

# 6. TIME-BASED METRICS

## Month-over-Month (MoM)

MoM Growth (%) = ((Current Month - Previous Month) / Previous Month) × 100

Compares performance between consecutive months.

## Quarter-over-Quarter (QoQ)

QoQ Growth (%) = ((Current Quarter - Previous Quarter) / Previous Quarter) × 100

Compares performance between consecutive quarters.

## Year-over-Year (YoY)

YoY Growth (%) = ((Current Period - Same Period Previous Year) / Same Period Previous Year) × 100

Compares performance against the equivalent period in the previous year.

IMPORTANT:
YoY comparisons help account for recurring seasonal patterns.

## Moving Average

Moving Average = Sum of Values in Period / Number of Periods

Example:
3-month moving average:

(January + February + March) / 3

Used to smooth short-term fluctuations and identify trends.

## Cumulative Total

Cumulative Total = Sum of Values From Beginning Through Current Period

Example:
Running total of monthly sales.

## Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)

CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / Number of Years) - 1

Multiply by 100 to express as a percentage.

Measures the annualized growth rate over multiple years.

IMPORTANT:
CAGR smooths growth into one annualized rate. It does not mean growth was identical every year.

# 7. OPERATIONAL METRICS

## Inventory Turnover

Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory

Average Inventory = (Beginning Inventory + Ending Inventory) / 2

Measures how frequently inventory is sold and replenished over a period.

## Inventory Days

Days Inventory Outstanding = (Average Inventory / COGS) × Number of Days in Period

Estimates how many days inventory is held.

## Order Fulfillment Rate

Fulfillment Rate (%) = (Orders Fulfilled / Total Orders) × 100

Measures the percentage of orders successfully fulfilled.

## On-Time Delivery Rate

On-Time Delivery (%) = (Orders Delivered On Time / Total Eligible Deliveries) × 100

Measures delivery reliability.

## Return Rate

Return Rate (%) = (Returned Orders or Units / Relevant Orders or Units) × 100

IMPORTANT:
Use the same measurement basis in numerator and denominator.

## Defect Rate

Defect Rate (%) = (Defective Units / Total Units Produced) × 100

Measures the proportion of products that fail quality requirements.

## Average Resolution Time

Average Resolution Time = Total Resolution Time / Number of Resolved Cases

Used in customer support and operations.

## Productivity

Productivity = Output / Input

Example:
Orders Processed / Employee Hours

Measures output relative to resources used.

# 8. STATISTICAL METRICS

## Mean

Mean = Sum of Values / Number of Values

Measures the arithmetic average.

## Weighted Average

Weighted Average = Sum(Value × Weight) / Sum(Weights)

Useful when observations have different levels of importance.

Example:
Calculating average selling price across products with different sales volumes.

## Median

The middle value in a sorted dataset.

Useful when data contains outliers or is skewed.

## Mode

The most frequently occurring value.

## Range

Range = Maximum - Minimum

Measures the overall spread of values.

## Variance

Population Variance = Sum of Squared Deviations / Population Size

Sample Variance = Sum of Squared Deviations / (Sample Size - 1)

Measures variability around the mean.

## Standard Deviation

Standard Deviation = Square Root of Variance

Measures how spread out values are around the mean.

## Interquartile Range (IQR)

IQR = Q3 - Q1

Measures the spread of the middle 50% of observations.

## Z-Score

Z = (Value - Mean) / Standard Deviation

Measures how many standard deviations a value is from the mean.

## Correlation Coefficient

Pearson's r ranges from -1 to +1.

- +1 = Perfect positive linear relationship.
- 0 = No linear relationship.
- -1 = Perfect negative linear relationship.

IMPORTANT:
Correlation does not prove causation.

# 9. BUSINESS PERFORMANCE & GROWTH METRICS

## Market Share

Market Share (%) = (Company Sales / Total Market Sales) × 100

Measures a company's portion of total market sales.

## Revenue Per Customer

Revenue Per Customer = Total Revenue / Number of Customers

Measures average revenue generated per customer over a defined period.

## Revenue Per Employee

Revenue Per Employee = Total Revenue / Number of Employees

Measures revenue generated per employee.

## Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)

ARPU = Total Revenue / Average Number of Active Users

Used frequently in subscription and technology businesses.

## Customer Concentration

Customer Revenue Share (%) = (Revenue From Customer / Total Revenue) × 100

Measures how much revenue depends on an individual customer.

High concentration may indicate business risk.

## Funnel Drop-Off Rate

Drop-Off Rate (%) = ((Starting Stage Count - Next Stage Count) / Starting Stage Count) × 100

Example:
1,000 visitors
200 checkout starts

Drop-Off = 80%

## Funnel Conversion Rate

Stage Conversion (%) = (Next Stage Count / Previous Stage Count) × 100

Measures progression between funnel stages.

# 10. IMPORTANT METRIC DEFINITIONS

Always establish:

1. NUMERATOR:
What is being counted?

2. DENOMINATOR:
What is the comparison base?

3. TIME PERIOD:
Daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly?

4. POPULATION:
Which customers, products or transactions are included?

5. EXCLUSIONS:
Are refunds, cancellations or test records excluded?

6. GRAIN:
Is the data measured per customer, order, transaction or product?

7. SOURCE:
Where does the data originate?

IMPORTANT:
Two departments can calculate the same KPI differently if their definitions aren't aligned.

# 11. COMMON METRICS INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

Q: What is the difference between revenue and profit?

A: Revenue is income generated from sales. Profit is what remains after deducting expenses.

Q: How do you calculate gross profit margin?

A: Gross profit divided by revenue, multiplied by 100.

Q: What is customer acquisition cost?

A: Total acquisition costs divided by the number of new customers acquired.

Q: What is customer churn?

A: The proportion of existing customers lost during a defined period.

Q: What is customer lifetime value?

A: An estimate of the value a customer generates over their relationship with a business.

Q: What is conversion rate?

A: The percentage of a defined population that completes a desired action.

Q: What is the difference between ROI and ROAS?

A: ROI measures return relative to investment cost. ROAS measures advertising-attributed revenue relative to advertising spend.

Q: What is month-over-month growth?

A: The percentage change between the current month and the previous month.

Q: Why use year-over-year comparisons?

A: They compare equivalent periods across years and can help account for seasonality.

Q: What is a weighted average?

A: An average that accounts for the different importance or frequency of observations.

Q: What is inventory turnover?

A: A measure of how frequently inventory is sold and replenished during a period.

Q: What is market share?

A: A company's sales as a percentage of total market sales.

# 12. FINAL FORMULA REMINDERS

FINANCIAL:
- Revenue = Price × Quantity
- Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS
- Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100
- Net Margin = Net Profit / Revenue × 100
- ROI = (Return - Investment Cost) / Investment Cost × 100
- Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs / Contribution Per Unit

CUSTOMER:
- CAC = Acquisition Costs / New Customers
- Churn = Customers Lost / Starting Customers × 100
- Retention = (Ending Customers - New Customers) / Starting Customers × 100
- AOV = Revenue / Orders
- CLV = Customer Value Per Period × Customer Lifespan

MARKETING:
- Conversion Rate = Conversions / Eligible Population × 100
- CTR = Clicks / Impressions × 100
- CPC = Advertising Spend / Clicks
- ROAS = Advertising Revenue / Advertising Spend

GROWTH:
- Percentage Change = (New - Old) / Old × 100
- MoM = Current Month vs Previous Month
- QoQ = Current Quarter vs Previous Quarter
- YoY = Current Period vs Same Period Last Year
- CAGR = (Ending / Beginning)^(1 / Years) - 1

STATISTICS:
- Mean = Sum / Count
- Range = Maximum - Minimum
- IQR = Q3 - Q1
- Z-Score = (Value - Mean) / Standard Deviation
- Weighted Average = Sum(Value × Weight) / Sum(Weights)

OPERATIONS:
- Inventory Turnover = COGS / Average Inventory
- Fulfillment Rate = Fulfilled Orders / Total Orders × 100
- Defect Rate = Defective Units / Total Units × 100
- Productivity = Output / Input
